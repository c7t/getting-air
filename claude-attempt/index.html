Great question — this is exactly the right thing to ask. Let me think through it carefully.

Why Phase 2 was stable but Phase 3 isn't

Phase 2: Fixed ellipse, body force drives horizontal flow. The solid-fluid boundary never moves. Standard bounce-back is exact for a stationary wall because it enforces u=0 at the surface — which is correct when u_wall = 0.

Phase 3: Card translates and rotates. Standard bounce-back still enforces u=0 at the surface — but now u_wall ≠ 0. The card surface is moving.

The moving boundary velocity has two components. For a point on the capsule at position r = (x - cx, y - cy) from the card center:

u_b = (vx - ω·ry,  vy + ω·rx)

With ω = 0.02 rad/step and tip distance r = A = 32 lu:

|u_rotation_at_tip| = ω · A = 0.02 × 32 = 0.64 lu/step

The LBM stability limit is ~0.3 lu/step. The rotational tip velocity alone is twice the stability limit, and without accounting for it in the bounce-back, we're injecting this as a spurious velocity error at the boundary on every step.

Compare to the correction magnitude vs. the distribution value:

Correction = 2·w_i·(e_i·u_b)/cs²  ≈  2·(1/9)·0.64·3  ≈  0.43
Equilibrium f  ≈  w_i  ≈  0.11
Relative error: ~390%

This is not a small perturbation — it's a 4× error at the boundary, every step. No amount of τ tuning or density clamping can fix a boundary condition that's systematically wrong.

Phase 2 was immune because u_b = 0 everywhere — stationary bounce-back is exact for a stationary wall. Phase 3 breaks it because the card's rotation creates tip velocities far above the correction threshold.

The fix: Moving Bounce-Back (Ladd 1994)

The standard result: for a fluid cell at (x,y) pulling from a solid in direction i, add a boundary velocity correction:

f_out[x,y,i] = f_col[x,y,opp(i)] + 2·w_i·(e_i·u_b)/cs²

where u_b is the card's velocity at the midpoint of the fluid-solid link (x - ½·e_i, y - ½·e_i).
